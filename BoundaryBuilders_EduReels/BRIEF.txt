1. Make ~/Downloads/BoundaryBuilders_EduReels/ with folders photos/raw, photos/jpg, photos/view, clips, scripts, audio/vo, audio/music, audio/sfx, frames, renders, qa, final, tools.

The Winter folder has no compiled binaries, so compile once: swiftc -O build.swift -o tools/build and swiftc -O audiofix.swift -o tools/audiofix.
3. The Winter pipeline you're reusing:
   a. An HTML/CSS animation on a 1080x1920 #stage with a deterministic JS seek(t) that sets every element's transform and opacity from time t, using the tween registry reg()/tw() with easings EO (cubic-out), EI, EIO, EOB (back-out) and EOX (expo-out).
   b. ElevenLabs voice-over, music and SFX (see AUDIO).
   c. audiofix: pitch-preserving stretch (AVAudioUnitTimePitch) plus silence trim, output .caf. Usage: tools/audiofix <in.mp3> <out.caf> <rate> <maxGapSec> <threshDb>, for example 1.2 0.25 -42. All five arguments are required.
   d. render.py drives headless Google Chrome over --remote-debugging-pipe (its CDP class). "plan" mode computes scene timings from the VO durations into a plan json and a clips json; "preview t1 t2" renders stills; "all" renders every frame at 30 fps; an env var selects the script.
   e. tools/build encode <framesDir> 30 <out.mp4> makes the H.264 video, then tools/build mux <video> <out> <clips.json> adds VO, music and SFX (each with start, volume and fadeOut) via AVMutableComposition.
   f. Verify by pulling frames out of the encoded MP4 with a small Swift AVAssetImageGenerator tool and looking at them. Write that tool.
4. Build ONE reusable template, edu.html, with scene types driven by ONE JSON script per video (scripts/NN_slug.json). Same seek(t) + tween approach, 30 fps, 1080x1920. Change render.py so VID=NN picks scripts/NN_*.json, and add a "cover" mode (renders the cover PNG) and a "qa" mode (see QA). Every frame depends only on t.
5. Scene types in edu.html:
   - hook: my real photo (full-bleed or band) at frame 0 with the series pill and small logo already on screen; headline words pop in from t=0.05.
   - photo: a real photo with a slow Ken Burns move, a headline or card, and optional callouts drawn on top (gold ring, arrow, dimension line with label, #267ECE wind or water arrows).
   - diagram: an animated SVG cross-section (see DIAGRAM SCENES).
   - card: the dark bottom card with ONE numbered gold badge row, gold tick row or grey-mark row.
   - myth: a wrong belief with a drawn X, struck through, then the fact with a gold tick.
   - clip: frames from the real paver video, with the band treatment.
   - end: the fully populated end card.
   - Transitions: the full-screen gold wipe with the near-black leading edge, at most 2-3 per video, each with a whoosh. A light hit sound on badges and numbers.
6. Clips: pull only the seconds you need from the paver clip as JPEG frames at 30 fps with a Swift AVAssetImageGenerator tool (tolerance zero) into clips/<name>/f_0000.jpg and so on. Load every clip frame as its own <img> at startup (so render.py's ready check waits for them to decode) and show the right one by toggling opacity in seek(t). Never swap img.src per frame. The clip's blurred fill can use one still from the middle of the segment. If a beat runs longer than its segment, first extend into the allowed seconds listed in the video spec, then slow to no less than 0.8x by repeating frames, then hold the last frame. Never loop, never run into excluded seconds, never show an empty or black frame. Mute the clip's own audio.

The ElevenLabs tools only work inside this chat, so a shell script can't make VO. tools/make.sh NN runs, in order: (1) promo check on the script; (2) VO check: sha1 of each beat's spoken text (asterisks stripped) compared with audio/vo/NN_hashes.json; if any beat's file is missing or its text changed, make.sh stops and prints exactly which beats need new VO; (3) audiofix; (4) plan; (5) render all; (6) encode; (7) mux; (8) cover; (9) QA frame extraction; (10) promo check on the on-screen strings that qa mode dumped. FACT LIST
Sources: USDA Forest Service, This Old House, ASCE 7 wind guidance, CMHA (Concrete Masonry & Hardscapes Association, formerly ICPI), Unilock, Mutual Materials, Allan Block, the Western Red Cedar Lumber Association, DarkSky International, Technical Safety BC, and the city guides for Burnaby, Coquitlam, Vancouver, Surrey and Maple Ridge.

Fences
- Wood posts rot first at ground level, where the soil supplies moisture and the air supplies oxygen. A leaning wood fence usually failed at grade, not at the bottom of the hole.
- Usual causes of a leaning fence: rot at grade; posts too shallow; soft, saturated soil (clay holding winter rain); wind on solid panels; heavy gates levering the hinge post; concrete set below grade or cupped so water pools at the post. Frost heave matters much less here (Vancouver's design frost depth is about 450 mm, roughly 18 in). Don't blame frost.
- Wind pressure goes with the square of wind speed, so twice the wind pushes about four times as hard. Solid fences (board-on-board, corrugated metal, solid composite) catch the full wind like a sail. A solid privacy fence needs strong posts and footings, not just nicer boards.
- Post depth rule of thumb: about 1/3 of the post's height plus about 6 in of gravel. That's about 2 ft minimum; about 3 ft is common for 6 ft privacy fences, gate posts, soft soil and windy sites. A rule of thumb, not code. Steel and composite systems follow the maker's footing spec.
- About 6 in of compacted 3/4 in crushed stone under the post lets water drain away.
- Crown the concrete slightly above grade and slope it away from the post so rain runs off.
- More concrete isn't automatically stronger. For wood posts, managing water at grade matters more.
- Posts are typically 6-8 ft apart, closer at corners.
- A kickboard or rot board (often a treated 2x6 or 2x8) keeps cedar boards off the soil and is the easy part to replace. Keep cedar out of dirt and mulch.
- Cedar resists rot, but it isn't rot-proof. In-ground posts should be ground-contact rated (CSA O80 UC4.1 treated) or steel.
- Cut ends absorb water about 11x faster than the sides, so post tops and board ends decay first. Post caps and sealed cuts help.
- Dark streaks running from fasteners on cedar come from the wrong fasteners reacting with the wood. Use stainless (316 near salt water) or hot-dip galvanized.
- Stain new cedar once it's surface-dry, within a couple of weeks, not 6-12 months later, on a dry day that meets the stain label's temperature and dry-time rules. Plan around dry days in Oct-Dec. Semi-transparent stain typically lasts 1-3 years on smooth cedar and 2-4 on rough-sawn.
- Board-on-board overlaps the boards so shrinkage doesn't open see-through gaps. It uses more lumber and is still solid to the wind.
- Aluminium can't rust because it contains no iron. Steel needs its galvanizing or powder coat intact; cut edges and scratches are where rust starts. Composite and vinyl need washing about twice a year; keep pressure washers under 1,500 psi or more than 12 in away.
- Fence heights are typically about 4 ft in front yards and about 6 ft in side and back yards, but every city differs: Burnaby 1.07 m anywhere and 1.8 m behind the required front yard; Coquitlam 1.3 m in front of the house face and 1.8 m elsewhere; Vancouver 1.2 m in front yards and about 6 ft side/rear permit-free; Surrey 1.2 m along streets and in front yards. Always say "check your city".
- Corner lots have a sightline triangle near the corner where fences, walls and hedges are limited to about 1 m (Burnaby: within 9 m of a street corner; Coquitlam: within 6 m).
- A fence on a retaining wall: some cities combine the heights. Coquitlam caps wall plus fence at 3.0 m, and Burnaby counts the part of a wall above the retained soil as fence.
- Only a BC Land Surveyor can confirm a property line.
- BC 1 Call is free. Request a locate at least 3 full business days before digging, at bc1c.ca, 1-800-474-6886 or *6886 from a cell. Fence posts and landscaping are listed reasons to call. It doesn't locate private lines (your own lighting, irrigation, or gas to a garage). Don't call it "the law".
- Pools need a non-climbable enclosure (Burnaby minimum 1.5 m). Check your city.

Gates
- Most sagging gates start at the hinge post, because a gate magnifies any post movement. The hinge post carries the gate like a lever, so it should be the strongest post on the run: bigger or steel, deeper, and set in concrete.
- A wood gate brace runs from the BOTTOM HINGE corner UP to the TOP LATCH corner, so it works in compression. Run the other way, it relies on screws in tension and the gate sags.
- An anti-sag cable runs the opposite way (top hinge corner to bottom latch corner, in tension), and tightening the turnbuckle lifts the latch side.
- Welded metal frames stay square longer than screwed wood frames, but they still sag if the hinge post moves.

Pavers
- Sinking pavers are usually a base problem: poor soil or base compaction, water in the base, too-thick bedding, washout, missing or loose edge restraint, soft clay with no separation fabric, or downspouts dumping onto the patio.
- CMHA minimum compacted base: 4 in for patios and walkways and 6 in for residential driveways, on IDEAL ground. CMHA's quick rule adds 50% of the minimum for each ideal condition a site lacks (strong subgrade, good drainage, no freezing). Wet clay isn't ideal, so plan at least 6 in for patios here, often more (about 8 in or more on soft, poorly drained clay). Driveways need more again: about 9-12 in or more on local clay (Unilock says 8-12 in for driveways). So "6 inches of base" is a sound patio spec on our clay, not a number for every job.
- The base is dense-graded crushed gravel (3/4 in minus, road-base type), not stone dust or river rock, compacted in lifts of 3-6 in with a proper plate compactor.
- Geotextile fabric over clay keeps the clay from pumping up into the base.
- The bedding layer is about 1 in, screeded flat and not compacted before laying. Extra bedding can't fix an uneven base; the unevenness shows through over time. Too thick a bedding layer is a known cause of settling. CMHA calls for washed concrete sand and never stone dust, screenings or mason sand.
- After laying, the pavers are plate-compacted (at least 2 passes; slab and textured products may need a protective pad or roller), then joint sand is swept in and compacted again until the joints are full.
- Every paver edge that isn't held by a curb or wall needs an edge restraint with at least 1 in of vertical contact. The base extends past the restraint by at least the base thickness (a 6 in base runs 6 in past it). Loose or missing restraints let pavers creep apart.
- Surfaces slope away from the house: CMHA's minimum is 1.5%, and about 2% (1/4 in per ft) is common.
- Polymeric sand needs dry pavers and about 24 h without rain.
- Joint weeds grow from windblown seeds lodged in the joints, not from below. Fabric under the base doesn't stop them; full joints and polymeric sand help.
- White haze on new pavers is efflorescence. It's natural and cosmetic, not a defect, and it fades (it typically stops within about 18-24 months).
- Aggressive pressure washing can blast the joint sand out, and the joints then need refilling.

Retaining walls
- Water is the most common cause of retaining wall failure. Water behind a wall adds hydrostatic pressure on top of the soil load, and saturated soil loses strength.
- Other causes: no compacted levelling pad, a first course that isn't buried, poorly compacted backfill, missing or short geogrid on taller walls, and loads on top that the wall wasn't designed for.
- Block wall section (Allan Block residential spec): a trench 18-24 in wide; at least 6 in of compacted wall rock as the levelling pad; clean crushed wall rock in the block cores and 12 in behind the wall; a perforated drain pipe at the back of the base, vented to daylight at least every 50 ft or tied into the drainage system (Allan Block calls for the pipe on reinforced walls, gravity walls over 4 ft and poorly drained sites; specs differ by block system); backfill compacted in lifts of 8 in or less.
- Bury the first course: Allan Block buries at least 6 in of block, or about 1 in per foot of wall height if that is more. Block makers' specs differ, so say "at least 6 in, deeper on taller walls" and "follow the block maker's spec".
- Each course of a block wall is set back into the hill (about 6-12 degrees). A wall leaning out toward the yard is a warning sign.
- The drain pipe must outlet to an approved system (Burnaby: plumbing permit and a storm sewer connection; Maple Ridge: tie into the on-lot drainage). Never onto a neighbour's property.
- Permits and engineers: the line is often around 4 ft (1.2 m), sometimes lower. Burnaby, Coquitlam and Surrey are around 1.2 m. Maple Ridge needs a permit over 0.6 m and sealed engineering over 1.0 m, with a 1.2 m max. Walls holding up driveways, slopes or foundations need an engineer.
- Clay and loads shrink the safe height. Allan Block's chart for unreinforced walls gives about 3 ft 6 in in clay versus about 5 ft 4 in in silty sand, and a driveway-type load drops the clay figure to about 1 ft 8 in. The chart excludes earthquake loads, and Metro Vancouver is seismic, so taller walls get engineered here.
- Two short walls stacked close together act like one tall wall, so terraces need real spacing. Burnaby wants the terrace at least as deep as the taller wall is high; Maple Ridge needs engineering for tiered walls less than 3.0 m apart.
- Warning signs: bulging, leaning forward, widening cracks, gaps between blocks, water weeping through the face, soil washing out at the base, caps dropping. Have a professional engineer look at a leaning or bulging wall.
- Wood walls are for short garden walls only (Maple Ridge allows them up to 0.6 m).

Landscaping
- BC Building Code 9.14.6.1 is performance-based: grade the site so water doesn't collect at or near the building and doesn't harm neighbours. The 2% figure is municipal guidance, not code.
- Downspouts dumping onto a patio, behind a wall or along a fence line feed the water problems that sink pavers, push walls and rot posts. Route roof water into the approved drainage for your lot and city.
- November is normally the wettest month in coastal BC (Vancouver gets about 180-200 mm).
- Artificial turf (installer practice, not code): dig out about 4-6 in, remove organics, compact the subgrade, lay a weed barrier, then 3-4 in of crushed 3/4 in minus base compacted in lifts. On wet clay, a deeper base or a subdrain is a judgement call. Slope it away from the house, nail the edges about every 6 in, brush the infill in when dry. Never put plastic under turf: it traps water, and turf drains through its backing into the base. Its drainage depends on the base. Turf is low maintenance, not no maintenance: leaves, rinsing, brushing, infill top-ups. Wet leaves left on turf hold moisture and feed moss.
- Landscape lighting (DarkSky International / IES): useful, targeted (shielded and aimed down), low-level, controlled (timers or sensors) and warm-coloured (commonly 3000K or lower). Light the path, not the neighbours.
- Path lights are commonly about 6-8 ft apart and staggered side to side; a straight double row looks like a runway. Low confidence, so always call it a rule of thumb.
- Plug-in low-voltage transformers go into a weather-protected GFCI outlet. Hard-wired outdoor circuits need an electrical permit. Burying cable is digging: BC 1 Call first.
- Fire pits: rules are municipal (Surrey and the Township of Langley ban wood fire pits, so gas or propane is usually the way to go). Backup topic only.

Never say any of these
- 6 in of base is right for every job.
- Thicker sand or bedding is better.
- Fabric stops joint weeds.
- Efflorescence means cheap pavers.
- Cedar never rots.
- Wait 6-12 months before staining, or stain damp wood.
- The gate brace runs from the top hinge to the bottom latch.
- Aluminium rusts, or powder-coated steel never rusts.
- More concrete is always stronger.
- Frost heave is the main cause of leaning fences here.
- Solid panels resist wind better.
- One fence height applies everywhere.
- Walls under 4 ft never need a permit.
- The Building Code requires 2%.
- Turf is zero maintenance, or can go on soil or plastic.
- Any lifespan in years as a guarantee.
- Any invented statistic ("80% of fences...").
